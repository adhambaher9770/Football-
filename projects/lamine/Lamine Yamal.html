Known for his exceptional skills, agility, and vision on the field, Lamine has quickly risen through the ranks to become one of the most promising talents in the sport. His dedication to training and his natural talent have made him a standout player, and many are excited to see how his career will unfold in the coming years.

The Story

Born July 13, 2007 in Esplugues de Llobregat, near Barcelona, to a Moroccan father and Equatorial Guinean mother. He started out at a local Catalan club, La Torreta, before his exceptional dribbling and ball control got him noticed as one of Catalonia's most promising young talents.

Early Days

In 2014, at just six or seven years old, he joined Barcelona's famous La Masia academy — the same system that produced Messi, Xavi, and Iniesta. He moved through the ranks quickly, often playing in age groups above his own, and his vision and flair made him a standout even among elite young prospects.

Breakthrough

The turning point came on April 29, 2023: he made his senior Barcelona debut in a La Liga match against Real Betis, which Barcelona won 4-0 — making him one of the club's youngest-ever first-team players. From there, he moved rapidly from academy prospect to regular starter, becoming central to Barcelona's attack with goals, assists, and progression against deep defenses.

Records and Titles

By the 2024-25 season he'd become a fixture — integral to a campaign that saw Barcelona win La Liga, the Copa del Rey, and the Supercopa de España, racking up 73 domestic appearances and 14 goals along the way. He's since become the youngest goalscorer in La Liga history and the youngest Barcelona player to feature in El Clásico, and now wears the club's iconic No. 10. Tribuna On the international stage, he's already a European champion and World Cup winner with Spain, and before turning 20 he'd made more than 150 appearances for Barcelona — with people already discussing him in the same breath as Mbappé, Bellingham, and Haaland.
