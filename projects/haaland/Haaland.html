Erling Haaland

The Story

Early life Erling Braut Haaland was born on July 21, 2000, in Leeds, England, while his father, Alf-Inge Haaland, was playing for Leeds United. Alf-Inge had worked his way up through Bryne's ranks in Norway before moving to England, but a serious knee injury — including a notorious tackle from Roy Keane — forced him to retire at just 30. The family then moved back to Alf-Inge's hometown of Bryne when Erling was around three years old. Erling Haaland +4 Bryne FK — the hometown club Erling joined Bryne's academy at age five, growing up in a small, unglamorous football town far from the spotlight. During the 2015–16 season he tore it up for Bryne's reserve team, scoring 18 goals in 14 games, and Bryne handed him his first-team debut three months before his 16th birthday. Notably, he didn't actually score in that senior breakthrough season — Hoffenheim even offered him a trial before he moved on. Erling Haaland Biography, Career, Stats, Age, Net Worth & Latest News - KhelaDesk English +4 Molde — learning from Solskjær In February 2017, Haaland signed with Molde FK, coached by Ole Gunnar Solskjær — a former international teammate of his father's. Under Solskjær, Haaland reportedly added around 15kg of muscle and worked heavily on his heading ability. His first season back was quiet (just two goals), but by his second season he was Molde's top scorer with 12 goals. Erling Haaland | Biography & Wiki | VAVEL USA +3 Red Bull Salzburg — the explosion In January 2019, Haaland moved to Red Bull Salzburg in Austria — the true start of his rise. After starting on the bench, he broke into the side by July and began scoring in almost every match, eventually notching three hat-tricks in the league. In the Champions League that season, he became the first teenager ever to score in five consecutive matches, netting eight goals in the group stage alone — including 62 goals in 67 appearances once transferred onward, a stretch that made every big club in Europe pay attention. Biography of Erling Haaland: path to fame, accolades and professional career +3 Borussia Dortmund and beyond Dortmund signed him in December 2019, and from there his path is the one most fans know: a move to Manchester City in 2022, where he's shattered Premier League scoring records, followed by leading Norway to the 2026 World Cup — their first appearance since 1998 — a run that ended in a hard-fought quarterfinal loss to England.
